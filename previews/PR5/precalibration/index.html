Precalibration

Precalibration keeps the parameter samples whose simulations pass through observational windows often enough (Mills et al. 2019; Wong et al. 2021).

  1. Constants. A Latin hypercube in 56 dimensions is mapped through the truncated priors (TruncatedPrior). With constant_design = :chunked_lhs (the default) every chunk is its own Latin hypercube. With :global_lhs a single design covers all samples, as in the R code. That uses about 9 GB at 20 million samples (finding F-11), and because the run usually stops early, only the first chunks of that design are ever evaluated.
  2. Forcings. Each of the 12 series is drawn around its centre (InverseWishartSampler): Σ ~ IW(ν, (ν−p−1)·diag(σ²)), then x ~ N(μ, Σ), with ν from inverse_wishart_dof. fR, fL, fA, fAw_fA, fC are then divided by their present-day value and GEOG has it subtracted. MultivariateTSampler draws from the same distribution (its exact multivariate-t marginal) in O(p) instead of O(p³).
  3. Windows. CO2: per 10 Myr step, the envelope of log10(co2) ± nsig·log10(co2_high/co2) over the proxies in that step (co2_windows). Temperature: Mills et al. (2019) mean ± nsig·(max/min − mean) (temperature_windows).
  4. % outbound. The fraction of steps outside the window; 1 if any step failed (CO2 = Inf) (legacy_percent_outbound).
  5. Acceptance. OutboundRules for CO2 and/or temperature, optionally a CretaceousWindowRule, combined with AllOf.

The engine (run_precalibration) processes chunks in parallel. Each chunk has its own StableRNG seeded from (seed, chunk) and results are consumed in chunk order, so a run gives the same samples on 1 or 64 threads and on any Julia version. With checkpoint_dir, finished chunks are saved and reused after an interruption.
